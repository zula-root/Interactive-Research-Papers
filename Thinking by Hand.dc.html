Why handwriting still wins

Does the way you write change what you understand?

Students who took lecture notes on laptops wrote more, but understood less, than students who wrote by hand. Mueller and Oppenheimer traced the difference to a habit: typists copied the lecture word for word, while handwriters had to decide what mattered. This page covers their 2014 paper and two newer studies that ask the same question about AI writing tools and about the brain itself.

No background in psychology is assumed. Each section stands alone.

Start here 2 min  ·  the core idea

Understanding comes from the effort of rewording

Psychologists have long distinguished two ways of handling new information. You can store it as it arrives, or you can process it: compare it with what you know, pick out the main point and put it in your own words. Processing is slower and harder, and it is what produces lasting understanding.

Most people type faster than they can listen, so a keyboard lets you store a lecture without processing it. Handwriting is too slow for that. A handwriter has to condense, and condensing is processing.

The same logic applies to tools that write for you. If the effort of choosing words is where the thinking happens, removing the effort may remove the thinking too.

Finding 01 3 min  ·  Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014

More notes, less understanding

Students watched short recorded talks and took notes either on a laptop or by hand. Laptop notes were longer and repeated more of the speaker's exact phrasing. On factual questions both groups scored about the same. On conceptual questions, which asked students to apply or explain ideas, the handwriters did better.

In a second study, laptop users were told not to copy word for word. They copied anyway. In a third, students were tested a week later after a chance to review their notes, and handwritten notes still produced better results. Take notes on the passage below and see how much you copy.

Finding 02 3 min  ·  Kosmyna et al., 2025

Essays written with AI left less behind

A team at the MIT Media Lab asked 54 adults to write short essays over several sessions while wearing EEG caps, which record electrical activity across the scalp. One group used ChatGPT, one used a search engine and one used nothing. Connectivity between brain regions was strongest in the unaided group and weakest in the ChatGPT group. Shortly after finishing, most ChatGPT users could not accurately quote a sentence from their own essay. Choose a group.

Finding 03 2 min  ·  Van der Weel & Van der Meer, 2024

The hand recruits more of the brain

Researchers in Norway recorded 36 university students with 256-electrode EEG nets while they wrote words with a digital pen or typed them. Handwriting produced widespread, coordinated activity across regions linked to memory and to encoding new information. Typing the same words did not. The authors attribute the difference to the fine, varied movements needed to form each letter, compared with pressing one key the same way every time. Write a word, then type it.

In conclusion 2 min  ·  what follows from it

The pen helps because it slows you down

The three studies point the same way: tools that make writing effortless can also make it thoughtless. The strength of that evidence varies, and the original result has been harder to repeat than its fame suggests.

Evidence since

Replications found a smaller gap

Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson (2019) and Urry and colleagues (2021) repeated the experiment. Laptop users again wrote more and copied more, but the difference in test scores was small or absent. How you take notes may matter more than what you take them with.

A practice

Draft first, then ask

Summarise in your own words before you consult anything else. A notebook makes that hard to avoid. In the MIT study, people who wrote unaided first and used ChatGPT afterwards stayed engaged; the reverse order did not recover it.

Limits

Small studies, early days

The AI study had 54 participants and has not been peer reviewed. The EEG handwriting study had 36 and measured brain activity, not learning. Both suggest where to look rather than settling the question.

Memory is also consolidated after you stop writing. See Sleep Rinses the Brain and Caffeine After Studying.
